# Citation trust rules

The bar is different for two kinds of claim in the same post. Know which one
you're writing before you decide whether a source is good enough.

## Two kinds of claim

**Factual / scientific claims** — statistics, research findings, "X causes
Y," dates, figures, anything presented as true. These need a Tier 1 source.
If you don't have one, cut the claim. Don't downgrade a Tier 2 source into
supporting it, and don't soften the language ("reportedly," "some say") as a
workaround — that's the same claim with a fig leaf on it.

**Sentiment / trend claims** — "people are excited about X," "the reaction on
X has been mixed," "this clip is going viral." These describe what a source
said or did, not an external fact, so a Tier 2 source is exactly the right
citation for them — a tweet proving people tweeted is solid.

## Tier 1 — trustworthy for factual/scientific claims

- Peer-reviewed journals and preprint servers with clear methodology
- `.gov`, `.edu`, and official standards-body sites
- Primary sources: the org's own press release, filing, or dataset
- Wire services and outlets with editorial/fact-checking standards
  (Reuters, AP, major national papers, established trade press)
- Official documentation from the company/product being discussed

## Tier 2 — usable only for sentiment/trend claims

- X/Twitter posts, Instagram posts, YouTube comments
- Personal blogs, forum threads, unverified aggregators
- A creator's own video/post describing their own opinion or experience

A Tier 2 source is never the citation for a factual or scientific claim, no
matter how confidently it's stated in the post — confidence isn't evidence.

## Mechanics

- **Inline**: cite at first mention of the claim, as a real hyperlink with
  a readable label — `[Reuters](https://...)`, not `[source](https://...)`
  or a bare URL.
- **References list**: one `##` section at the very bottom, numbered in
  order of first appearance in the body:
  `1. [Publisher or Title](url) — what this source supports`.
- **1:1 match**: every inline citation has exactly one References entry, and
  every References entry is cited at least once inline. If a source stops
  being used during editing, remove it from both places, not just one.
- **Dedupe by URL**, not by label — the same article cited twice gets one
  References entry, referenced twice inline.
